In the garden

Most plant labels stop at the name this one keeps going

A card in the ground tells you what the plant needs, what it feeds, and whether it would work where you garden. Scan the code and the rest of it comes home with you.

In your hand

The code opens the other half

The card holds what you need standing in front of the plant. The page holds what you want later — at home, deciding whether to plant it, and where.

Scan
What you get

Two halves of one answer

On the card

  • A photograph of the plant in the ground, not in a pot
  • Common and scientific name
  • Light, water, soil, mature size, bloom colour and months
  • Why it is worth growing, in two to four points
  • Where it is native, and which pollinators it draws

On the phone

  • Everything printed on the card
  • What it feeds — birds, butterflies, specialist bees
  • Plants that grow well beside it
  • How to plant it, step by step
  • Detail photographs: leaf, seed head, bark, habit
  • A list you can save as you walk the garden

Get in touch

The same system, a different plant list

Cards and pages are generated from one plant dataset, so a group with its own species list can produce its own set. If yours is thinking about something like this — or you have spotted something wrong on a plant page — send a message.

It helps to say roughly how many species you have in mind and when you would need them.
